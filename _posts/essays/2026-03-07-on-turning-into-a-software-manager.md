People talk about the Dario Amodei statement that "90% of code will be written by machines by Sept. 2025"[^1] as though it needed some sage-like ability to predict the future. But if you had shown me what Claude Code would look like in Dec. 2025, it would have been obvious that most code is about to start being written by machines.

Now, everyone has access to these tools for $20/month, yet there are still plenty of holdouts who feel it's not nearly as good as human coders (and, in predicting straight line future performance, likely never will be). There's a few reasons that appear for why this opinion is held:
- Sometimes it's just applying uneven standards to human and LLM-written code — for example, not acknowledging that it's actually a very good thing to have a partly working example written in 5 minutes as opposed to 10 hours.
- Sometimes it's focusing on immorality or bad ethics of some tech industry leaders, and using that as proof that LLMs couldn't possibly be that effective
- Sometimes it's conflating intelligence and consciousness — "it is obvious that LLMs aren't conscious, so I don't know why you're saying they're intelligent."

But I think the biggest resistance, often unacknowledged, is the feeling that if it were true, it would mean losing your job. 

I don't mean just losing your paycheck, but losing your place and your designation of value in the economy. It's an unfortunate fact that many engineers place much of their self worth in their job, and it can be more subtle than the annoying guy who does everything he can to remind you how smart he is. For many people who excelled at school, went to graduate school, and are employed in their current specialty, they've discovered that the world rewards them based on their ability to "be smart" or "solve problems". Until this year, any business or entity that had difficult problems needed to find some human to pay to think about them for awhile and communicate or build a solution to that problem.

It is going to be uncomfortable for many people to address the fact that this is no longer true, and I'm sure Google will start to get more searches like "Does having an EE Ph.D help in becoming an electrician?"[^2].
As we see more open flailing and thrashing of people, both in person and online, it will be good to have some empathy and remember that this is getting dumped on all of us together at the same time.
